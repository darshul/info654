Madame Georges Charpentier and Her Children, Georgette-Berthe and Paul-Emile-Charles (Auguste Renoir, France, 1878)

This is the home image of my gallery. I selected this one to be my home image due to the inviting nature of the painting. In all honesty, it is not my favorite but it was a horizontal image with a border instilled (that I liked). Anyway... this is an image of a wealthy patron looking down at her two children as one rests on their black and white dog. The painting leans more to the center-left while the right side is adorned with interior embellishments (such as, a chair and a vase containing flowers). The painting has a very understated undertone with blacks and warmer colors. But, the children's dresses--that are painted in a beautiful pastel blue--stand out against the muted backdrop.

Adoring angel (Italy, 15th century)

This figure likely originates from a scene of the Nativity. I'm drawn to this image likely due to its royal purple background in contrast with the warmer features of the figurine. Although depicting a Nativity scene, the angel looks like it's in desperation and pleaing to the Heavens above. There is something very melancholic and a kind of... responsive nature that I have when looking at the angel.

Flask-shaped bottle decorated with peonies (Korea, late 15th century)

I absolutely adore Korean ceramics--more specifically, buncheongware. During my undergrad, I took a class in East Asian art which led me to this specific ceramic style. One story that stuck with me was how my professor had told the class that the kawaii/Harajuku aesthetic in Japan started from the country kidnapping Korean ceramicists (as they were in the midst of buncheongware)!!
Regardless, I absolutely adore this bottle. The obvious human etching at the spout of the bottle creates a more homelike admiration for the piece. Also, peonies are one of my favorite flowers.

Incense burner with feline head (Bolivia, 500-1000CE)

How cute! This is one of those kind of objects that I wish I owned... lol. As stated on The MET website (which is viewable with a click of this image), this is a ceramic vessel created by (a) Tiwanaku artist(s). It's described as a very restrained ceramic that contained soot which led to its conclusion of being an incense burner. I can see its restrained nature due to the details and near perfection of the ceramic (in comparison to the more humble buncheongware in the above image). I believe that I was drawn to the ceramic because of the feline's face. The feline looks like it's trying to be scary but looks somewhat childlike--which I find adorable. The feline's face, in my opinion, resembles a Disney cartoon character.
